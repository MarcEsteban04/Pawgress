Sprint 05 · Brand
What the brand
has to survive
Three directions follow. Each is a complete answer, and each is judged against the same six constraints — they come from the product, not from taste.
1
A low number must not feel like a verdict. Students will see “Genetics 42%”. The brand decides whether that reads as information or as failure. This is the single hardest thing to get right.
2
Subject colour must never read as status colour. A red subject would look like a failed one. Subjects get a muted, low-chroma family; status keeps the saturated end to itself.
3
Legible on a cheap phone at low brightness. WCAG AA on body text, 16 px floor, and never colour alone — every state pairs with an icon and a label.
4
Two registers, one voice. Trustworthy enough for schoolwork on a laptop, warm enough to open at 11pm before an exam. Neither a toy nor a spreadsheet.
5
The mascot celebrates; it never apologises. It appears on progress, streaks and empty states. It is absent from every error and every failure — no cute animal on a broken upload.
6
Type survives long words in two languages. Mixed English and Filipino material is likely, so the type ramp is tested on long compounds, not on “Lorem ipsum”.
Constraint 2, made concrete
subject colours — muted, interchangeable, never semantic
status colours — saturated, reserved, meaningful
ready  ·  needs work  ·  failed
How to read the three boards
Every direction shows the same things in its own language: logo lockup, mascot, type ramp, palette, icon style, app icon and favicon — and then the same mastery card, because that is the component the whole product hangs on. Compare that card first.
